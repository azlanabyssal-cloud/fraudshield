ABOUT

A scam hit my family. I built this.

FraudShield is my Community Service Project at G. Pulla Reddy Engineering College, Kurnool. Its first job is simple: tell you what to do in the first hour after a scam.

Why this exists

It started with one phone call.

A caller said he was CBI. He told my uncle his Aadhaar was tied to a drug case and kept him on a video call for six hours. My uncle moved ₹3.4 lakh before my cousin stepped in.

My uncle is a retired government employee. Educated. Careful. It worked on him anyway.

Intelligence was not the gap. Information was. The steps that help are free and public, but they sit in PDFs written in legal language. I put them where people will read them: the first-hour steps, the 1930 helpline, and the scripts criminals run.

On the ground

I went to the doors and the shops.

A website does not reach someone who never searched for it. So I went to Munagalapadu, Andhra Pradesh and talked to people in their homes and shops about digital fraud and what to do when it happens. First visit: Sunday 3 May 2026.

This is my Community Service Project at G. Pulla Reddy Engineering College, Kurnool. The programme asks for a community survey, an awareness campaign and a main project. This site is the main project.

Standards

What I stand behind. What I do not claim.

Every number has a source.

All statistics sit in one data file, each with its source and a status. If a page disagrees with that file, the build fails. Check them on The Data page.

Guidance, not a verdict.

The assistant never tells you a message is safe. It names the warning signs and the next step. I am building a test set of real messages to measure how often it is right, and I will publish the result, good or bad.

Your messages stay in your browser.

No accounts. No analytics. Text and screenshots you give the assistant are processed on your device. Pages still load fonts from Google and some photos from Unsplash.

Independent.

Not a government body. Not legal advice. The official routes are the 1930 helpline and cybercrime.gov.in.

Found an error? Tell me.

Every figure lists its source, so mistakes can be found and fixed. The code and the data are open.
